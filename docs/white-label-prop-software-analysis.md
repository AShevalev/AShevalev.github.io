# Apertos: White-Label Prop Firm Software
## Name recommendation and industry analysis (pure SaaS)

**Audience:** founder brief for a B2B technology company that sells the operating stack other prop firms rebrand and run.  
**Model:** pure SaaS (setup + monthly; clients keep 100% of challenge revenue and profit splits).  
**Date:** August 2026  
**Related retail brand:** Verodus (keep separate from the B2B brand).

---

## 1. Recommended name: Apertos

**Apertos** (uh-PER-toss), from Latin *apertus* — open, uncovered.

It names the company you are actually starting: a software vendor, not another funded-trader brand. It does not start with “Prop,” does not sound like a challenge product, and does not collide with the crowded retail names (FTMO, FundedNext, Apex, Topstep) or the current B2B set (YourPropFirm, Tickblaze, Axcera, Nexus Prop, PropForge, Propriotec, Match-Trade).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pronunciation** | uh-PER-toss |
| **Tagline** | You keep 100%. We run the engine. |
| **Alt taglines** | Open infrastructure for prop firms. / One system of record for rules, risk, and payouts. |
| **Domain** | `apertos.io` has no public DNS as of this writing (likely registrable). `apertos.com` resolves to a parking IP and may be buyable. |
| **Why it fits pure SaaS** | “Open” is the opposite of revenue-share lock-in: transparent pricing, data export, API-first, no cut of client economics. |

### Why not call it Verodus

Verodus is a retail prop brand. Selling white-label software under the same name puts you in direct competition with your customers. Some vendors (PropForge) lean into “we run a live firm on this engine.” That can work as *proof*, not as the product name. Use Verodus as the lighthouse customer: “the stack Verodus runs on.” Do not put Verodus on the invoice.

### Shortlist (if Apertos is taken or feels wrong)

| Name | Angle | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Ballast** | Best metaphor: the weight that keeps a ship upright (risk/payout stability) | `ballast.com` is taken; `.tech` is parked |
| **Veralis** | Sister brand to Verodus (“true / valid rules”) | Buyers may still treat you as a competing retail firm |
| **Nodus** | Latin for knot/node — one connection point for rules, accounts, payouts | Too close, phonetically, to **Nexus Prop** |
| **Foundry** | Operator-built: firms are forged here | Generic in tech; use only with a distinctive domain |

Do a trademark search in your incorporation jurisdiction before registering. This brief is a brand recommendation, not a clearance opinion.

## 2. What you are building (do not mix the two businesses)

There are two different companies people call “white label prop”:

1. **A white-label prop firm** — you lease someone else’s stack, put Verodus (or another brand) on it, sell challenges, and take payout risk.
2. **A white-label prop software company** — you *sell* that stack as SaaS to other operators. That is this plan.

Apertos is (2). Pure SaaS means:

- You charge setup + monthly (and maybe light usage).
- Clients keep 100% of challenge fees and trader profit splits.
- You do **not** fund accounts or cover payouts.
- You win on risk-engine quality, uptime, platforms, admin tooling, and total cost of ownership — not on providing capital.

That is the higher-margin, lower-balance-sheet path. It also shrinks the easiest buyer: under-capitalized influencers who want a $3k setup and a revenue-share partner to absorb payouts (FPFX / PropAccount, PropSuite). Your buyers are operators who can fund their own book: existing firms fleeing rev-share, brokers adding a prop vertical, multi-brand groups, and well-capitalized new founders.

## 3. End market: retail prop firms (your customers’ market)

There is no audited global ledger for retail prop. Treat every headline number as a **range with a method**, not a fact.

### 3.1 Size — three ways to read it

| Lens | 2026 figure | What it actually counts | Source quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operator net revenue (conservative)** | ~**$850M**, +45% YoY from ~$585M in 2025 | Challenge fees + firm share of profit splits, after refunds/payouts | Track360 bottom-up estimate (July 2026). Vendor with an affiliate product in this vertical. |
| **Gross challenge fee flow** | ~**$3B** | 12M purchases × ~$250 average fee, *before* refunds, resets, and payouts | Same Track360 inputs |
| **Broader / looser estimates** | **$1–4.5B** primary; some coverage says **$6.7–12B** or “low tens of billions” | Mixes challenge fees, allocated capital, brokerage downstream, directories of 2,000+ “firms” | PropFirmsTech, Gitnux, AIFO. Scope is usually undefined. |

**Harder numbers you can actually cite:**

- **FTMO parent OMHC:** CZK 6.84B ≈ **$329M revenue in 2024**, +53% YoY; ~$62.5M net profit (Finance Magnates, Czech filings). This includes non-prop assets (real estate). Track360 still puts FTMO-the-prop-firm at **$180–210M**.
- **FundedNext:** revenue **exceeded $100M in 2024** (TradeInformer, unnamed source; not a filing).
- Track360 top 5 (FTMO, FundedNext, The 5%ers, Apex, Topstep): about **62% of trader acquisition** and ~**$530M of the $850M**.

Reconciliation: if FTMO’s holding company prints $329M and FundedNext is already $100M+, an $850M *industry* total is tight unless OMHC’s figure is heavily non-prop or Track360 is netting more aggressively than filings. Use **$0.85–2B** as the working band for retail prop *operator* revenue, and **~$3B** as gross evaluation spend. Do not plan Apertos as if the end market were $10B+.

### 3.2 Structure

| Metric | 2026 snapshot | Implication for Apertos |
|---|---|---|
| Active acquiring firms | **~120–150** (Track360); ~230 broader brands | Buyer universe is small. This is a specialist sales motion, not a self-serve PLG ocean. |
| Peak firm count | **220+** in 2023 | 80–100 closures (Finance Magnates / Track360, Feb 2024–late 2025). New logos still appear, but net adds are slower. |
| Challenge pass rate | **5–14%** funded; ~**7%** of buyers ever paid | Your clients’ unit economics are brutal. Software that mis-enforces rules or leaks fraud kills them — and then you. |
| Repeat purchases | **30–40%** of failed buyers reset/re-buy within 90 days | Challenge engines, retries, and affiliate attribution are core product, not extras. |
| Affiliate share of acquisition | **40–50%** of new purchases; $40–$80 CPA or 10–25% fee rev-share | Most WL CRMs ship a thin affiliate module. A serious partner layer is a differentiation lever (or a Track360-style add-on). |
| Platform mix | MT-only firms fell from ~**85%** (2023) to **<30%** | Multi-platform is table stakes: cTrader, DXtrade, Match-Trader, TradeLocker, plus Rithmic/NinjaTrader for futures. |
| Profit splits | **80–90%** advertised (up from 70–80% in 2022) | Operators compete on trader-facing terms; they need software that can actually pay weekly/on-demand without breaking. |

### 3.3 Track360 revenue-mix (treat as directional)

Of their $850M:

- Challenge fees ~**42%**
- Firm-side profit-split economics ~**38%**
- Affiliate commissions (a *cost*) ~**15%**
- White-label / API / tools ~**5%** → ~**$42M**

That 5% is the closest published proxy for **tech spend inside the vertical**. It is not a clean SaaS TAM (see §4).

### 3.4 Regulatory overlay (it shapes software requirements)

- **CFTC v. MyForexFunds (2023):** ~$310M fees from ~135k customers alleged. US regulators will reach simulated-payout models.
- **MetaQuotes (Feb 2024):** MT4/MT5 pulled from US-facing grey-label prop. Survivors migrated or geo-blocked the US.
- **2026:** no dedicated “prop license” in major jurisdictions. Singapore / UAE / Curaçao registrations are the de facto tier. Marketing rules (FCA PS22-10, ESMA, CySEC) attach when the funnel touches CFDs.
- Product implication: KYC/AML, geo-fencing, audit trails for every breach and payout, and **data portability** if a platform vendor changes policy again.

## 4. Your market: white-label software (B2B)

### 4.1 How big is the software layer?

Two estimates, both imperfect:

**Top-down.** If operators spend ~5% of $850M on white-label/tech, that is **~$40–45M/year**. Institutional/broker white-label (banks, brokers, crypto exchanges launching a branded prop vertical) is the expansion story: Track360 flags **$50–100M incremental by 2028**.

**Bottom-up (pure SaaS only).**

| Assumption | Conservative | Base | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Live paying WL clients, industry-wide | 80 | 150 | 250 |
| Blended ARPU | $3,500/mo | $6,000/mo | $9,000/mo |
| **Category recurring revenue** | **~$3.4M** | **~$11M** | **~$27M** |

Setup fees and usage add a bit. **Capital-backed** vendors (FPFX, PropSuite) sit on a *much* larger pool because they take 50–70% of client GMV — that is not your pool in a pure SaaS model.

Vendor **client-count claims** (65, 80, 95, 150+) cannot be added together. They overlap, include churned “launches,” and exceed the 120–150 acquiring-firm population. Treat them as marketing, then demand live references.

**Planning number for Apertos:** the pure-SaaS category is **high-single-digit to low-tens of millions ARR today**, sitting under a **$0.85–2B** operator market that still grows, but consolidates. A new vendor that reaches **40–60 solid clients** at **$4–7k/month** is a **$2–5M ARR** business. That is attractive software economics. It is not a venture-scale TAM unless you also win brokers, multi-brand groups, and futures/crypto, or later add a capital product.

### 4.2 Why demand exists anyway

- Building equivalent in-house is still **$100k–$500k+** and **6–18 months** (industry vendor ranges; Track360 says six-to-seven figures / 9–18 months).
- Long-tail firms (the $320M not captured by the top 5) cannot staff a risk-engine team.
- Existing firms **migrate** after payout disputes, outages, rev-share regret, or platform lock-in.
- Brokers and educators still launch prop verticals; they buy turnkey.
- Futures and multi-asset are still under-served relative to FX/CFD-first stacks.

### 5.2 Capital-backed / rev-share (not your model — they steal the easy logos)

| Vendor | Deal | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|

Your competitive line against these: *“At $200k/month challenge revenue, a 50% split is $100k/month. Apertos is a mid-four-figure subscription. Keep the book.”*

### 5.3 What the category actually competes on

1. **Risk engine** — configurability and *identical* enforcement in trading vs CRM (Nexus’s explicit pitch). Daily/overall drawdown types, trailing vs static, consistency, news/lot filters, weekend holding, copy-trading/fraud.
2. **Platform coverage** — FX/CFD is crowded; **futures (Rithmic, NinjaTrader, Tradovate, ProjectX)** and true multi-asset still differentiate.
3. **Payouts** — status machine, crypto+fiat, certificates, dispute evidence. Reputation of *your clients* depends on this.
4. **Commercial terms** — no rev share, data export, exit/migration, SLA.
5. **Trust** — live references, uptime, Trustpilot (weak across most B2B vendors).

Do not compete on “launch in 7 days” or lowest price. Tradexis and PropAccount already own those stories.

## 6. Pricing reality and Apertos unit economics (pure SaaS)

### 6.1 Market price bands (2026)

| Component | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Setup / branding | $0–$50k (often $0–$15k for mid-market SaaS) |
| Monthly platform | **$2,500–$15,000+** (mid-market cluster **$3–8k**) |
| Usage add-ons | $1–$10 per account, or $20–$35 per *active* trader (Nexus) |
| Passed through | MT5/cTrader licenses and data (licensed MT often cited **$10–15k** upfront + **$7–12k/mo**), KYC per check, PSP **3–8%** (high-risk) |

Published anchors: Match-Trade $2.5–5k/mo; Propriotec $3–15k/mo by accounts; Nexus $5k + $20–35/active trader.

### 6.2 Suggested Apertos packaging (to sit in the cluster, not under it)

| Tier | Monthly | Fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | $3,500 | ≤500 active accounts | Full white-label, challenge engine, risk, KYC/payouts connectors, 2 platforms |
| **Growth** | $6,500 | ≤2,000 accounts | Affiliate/IB module depth, extra platforms, staging + SLA |
| **Scale** | $12,000 | ≤8,000 accounts | Dedicated success, custom rules, priority roadmap |
| **Enterprise** | Quote | Multi-brand / broker | SSO, dedicated tenancy, custom MSA |

- **Setup:** $8,000 Launch / $15,000 Growth (waive if annual prepay).
- **No revenue share. No per-challenge fee.** Optional: overage $2/active account above the tier cap so you are not trapped like a flat $3k on 10k accounts.
- **Positioning line:** “Keep 100% of evaluation revenue. Pay for software, not a silent partner.”

### 6.3 Revenue sketch (18–24 months, not a forecast)

| | Conservative | Base | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paying clients | 20 | 40 | 60 |
| Blended ARPU | $4,500/mo | $6,000/mo | $7,500/mo |
| **ARR** | **$1.1M** | **$2.9M** | **$5.4M** |
| Plus setup (year 1) | ~$0.2M | ~$0.4M | ~$0.6M |

Costs that actually bite: 24/7 support, cloud, platform bridges, payment/KYC partner minimums, and senior engineers on the risk engine. After ~15–20 live firms, incremental gross margin should be high. Before that, this is a product company, not a lifestyle SaaS.

Cold-start: Verodus as customer zero. Then 3–5 **named** reference firms (ideally one futures, one FX, one multi-brand). Without references, you will not take logos from YourPropFirm or FPFX.

## 7. Strategic position (Apertos + Verodus)

You already have a live retail brand and custom work (site, challenge/sim, KYC, MetaTrader-related pieces). That is the PropForge story, with a cleaner split:

| Layer | Who owns it |
|---|---|
| Retail brand, trader acquisition, payouts, rules as *product* | **Verodus** |
| Challenge engine, real-time risk, multi-platform bridge, admin CRM, payout automation, white-label branding | **Apertos** (product you also sell) |
| Unique analytics, Monte Carlo, competitions, frontend experiments | Keep proprietary on Verodus or as optional Apertos modules |

**Do not** lead sales with “we also run Verodus.” Lead with engine, data ownership, and price. Disclose the related firm when asked, and offer a reference call with the Verodus operator, not a marketing page that makes buyers feel they are funding a rival.

**Highest-ROI wedge vs. the table in §5:**

1. Risk engine that is configurable *and* has a single authority (no CRM vs terminal disagreement).
2. Futures + FX in one tenancy (the gap Match-Trade and Propriotec leave open; Tickblaze/Trade Tech already occupy it — you need a real Rithmic path or you stay FX-only).
3. Published pricing + contract that states **data export and 30-day migration assistance**.
4. Affiliate/IB that can do CPA + rev-share + S2S, which most WL CRMs fake.

## 8. Risks (for a new pure-SaaS vendor)

- **Tiny buyer set.** 120–150 real acquirers, top 5 do not buy your CRM, and claimed competitor counts already “cover” the long tail.
- **Trust.** One bad liquidation bug burns three client brands and your category reputation.
- **Incumbent integrations.** Platform bridges are the moat. Budget them as the product, not as a phase-2 extra.
- **Rev-share competitors** will out-sell you to broke founders. Let them. Your ICP is operators who already understand payout liability.
- **Regulation.** If your clients are dinged, you inherit the support and geo-fencing workload even if you are “just software.”
- **Build cost.** A credible v1 is still many months of specialized engineering. White-label *for them* was the shortcut; white-label *as you* is the hard road.

---

## 9. Bottom line

- **Name the B2B company Apertos.** Keep Verodus as the retail proof, not the product.
- **End market** is real and still growing, but **concentrated**: work with **$0.85–2B** operator revenue, ~**12M** challenges/year, **120–150** serious firms, top 5 at ~**60%+**.
- **Your category** (pure SaaS white-label) is a **~$5–25M** recurring pool today, not a slice of $850M. Capital-backed vendors capture more GMV and the naive founders.
- **Competitors to study first:** YourPropFirm, Tickblaze, Trade Tech, Match-Trade, Propriotec, Axcera, Nexus Prop, PropForge. **FPFX/PropAccount** is the alternative *model*, not a feature peer.
- **Price** in the **$3.5–12k/month** band, no rev share, published tiers. **$2–5M ARR** at 40–60 clients is a realistic success case, not a conservative floor.
- Win on **risk-engine truth, multi-platform (especially futures), data ownership, and references** — not on launch-in-a-week theater.
