## 1. There is no single company to sell 60% of

Joe talked about buying “the majority of the company.” On paper there are **four** companies and **three** independent contracts.

| If they buy | They get | They do not get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60% of **Capital** | Current cash receipts / Canadian processor | Software, domain, raw data |
| 60% of **Dubai FZ** | Domain, raw data, CRM operation | Proprietary code (only a revocable sublicense); Canadian payment rails |
| 60% of **Holding** | Third-party software sublicense position | Proprietary stack; payments |
| 60% of **1591011 B.C. Ltd.** | The actual system | Domain, trader relationships, payment licences |

A 60% deal that is not a **coordinated purchase of all four**, or a pre-closing reorg into one HoldCo, leaves Joe able to walk the operating business off the software (revocable sublicense) or walk the software off the brand.

Joe already said system ownership is “one of the most important parts” and that they do not want to set it up twice. Expect his counsel to ask for a structure chart **with percentages** and a statement of who can terminate the sublicense.

## 2. Dubai cannot legally put TAMS/TradeMap on the software by itself

Both software sublicenses forbid **re-sublicensing** (s.2.2). Integration “TradeMap in front, Verodus backend” is a third-party use. Before Kim starts work, get written permission from **1591011 B.C. Ltd.** (Chun Chan) and check Holding’s upstream third-party licences.

## 3. Data story on the call vs the contracts

If you agree to “combined data” in a trial side letter, you may be giving away Dubai’s most important asset before a price is set. Keep trial data ownership explicit: whose traders, whose book, whose licence to train models.
