Valuation of the three companies, and what is a reasonable option premium

25 August 2026. Internal working paper for Alexander, Kim, and Chun. Not an offer, not a fairness opinion, not for Joe. Companion to the acquisition-comps note. Perimeter: Verodus Capital Inc., Verodus L.L.C.-FZ, and 1591011 B.C. Ltd. Software in. Kim stays. Chun signs. Do not mention a holding company.

Standing value today: $1.2 million. Ask $900,000 cash for 60% (implied $1.5 million). Take $750,000. Floor $660,000. Joe’s $600,000 example is below the floor unless a volume ratchet is written in.

Option premium that is actually reasonable: $75,000 for a 6–9 month call at a $900,000 strike, plus a separate $50,000 earned TAMS integration fee. Day-one cash = $125,000. Premium credits dollar-for-dollar against $900,000 if he closes; you keep it if he walks.

The earlier $75,000–$150,000 band was the right shape (it is option money, not a setup fee). The centre of what a first counterparty will pay, and what Black-Scholes-shaped maths supports on a 25% out-of-the-money 9-month call, is $75,000, not $150,000 stacked on top of integration. $150,000 is the price of a twelve-month freeze into his volume with no ratchet, or of 80% implied volatility. Do not open there.

1. What this paper is for

Two different instruments are being mixed in the same conversation. One is what the three companies are worth today, standing alone, ads-normalised, Kim still in the chair. The other is what Joe should pay now for the right to buy 60% later at a frozen price. Confusing them is how you either donate a free call or look like you are extracting cash from a maybe-partner.

This note rebuilds the $1.2 million from the live book with sensitivities, shows why $900,000 for 60% is a control-and-freeze ask rather than a pro-rata sale, then prices the freeze as a call option three ways: scenario expected value, private-market custom (exclusivity / option money / break fees), and a Black-Scholes cross-check. The recommended premium is the overlap of those three, cut for the fact that a large part of the upside is his volume thesis.

Cash still lands at Verodus Capital Inc. Not a personal cheque to Alexander. Do not put any of these figures in the org-chart email.

4. Three ways to $1.2 million, plus a four-year cheque

Owner-operated businesses under ~$5 million revenue trade on seller’s discretionary earnings, not on “fintech EBITDA.” BizBuySell’s 2024 insight report: average SDE multiple ~2.57× across all industries; typical band 2–4×. Sub-12-month history knocks a turn or more off. Verodus is a cash-generative evaluation shop with one IT person, three affiliates, and four months of operations. It is not a restaurant (1.7–3×) and not a contractual SaaS book.

5. Why 60% is $900,000, not $720,000

Sixty percent of $1.2 million is $720,000. That would be a fair split if Joe were buying a silent stake after a year of joint volume. He is not. He asked to lock the maths on day one so Verodus cannot re-trade after the book matures, and his default is majority (51%, “normally 60%”). That is a control purchase plus a call on future equity value.

  • Perimeter. The $900,000 is 60% of Capital and LLC-FZ and 1591011, same close. Without 1591011, Dubai’s licence is revocable and non-transferable. Without Kim contracted to stay, the Java stack has no IT.
  • Cash, not Hub. Consideration is dollars. Not Hub equity, not education IP, not “we inject the network.” If he will only do $600,000 cash, the gap is a ratchet on his volume, not a gift.

The $900,000 strike is already 25% above today’s pro-rata 60%. That matters for the option: at today’s $1.2 million, a call to buy 60% for $900,000 is out of the money. He only exercises if the book is worth more than $1.5 million when the window opens. That is the right shape. A strike of $600,000 would be in-the-money on day one, which is why his example is not a freeze, it is a discount.

6. The instrument: a call, not a delayed sale

Joe’s own sequence on the call was integrate → trial → 6–12 months of volume → then majority. A day-one number contradicts that sequence. He wants the sequence for risk and the lock for price. That is a European-style call on 60% of the perimeter:

  • Strike: $900,000 cash (ask). Not Hub. Not IP.
  • Underlying: 60% of the three companies, Kim contracted to stay, Chun signing 1591011.
  • Exercise: only if named gates are met, in a short window (for example 90 days after a successful trial, or a 6–9 month long-stop from signing).
  • If trial fails: option lapses. Nobody pays. Nobody is forced to sell.

Do not sign a binding SPA that closes “after the trial.” He will demand a MAC / walk right, and you will have given him the price with none of the volume. A refundable deposit toward $900,000 looks like cash and is not: he claws it back. A no-shop with no cash is a free option plus a lock-up of other buyers.

Give a ceiling, not a floor. “If you elect to buy 60% in the window, the price is $900,000 cash.” You do not promise to sell if he never elects. You do not let him elect after the window. If he wants the freeze to run into the 6–12 month volume phase with no expiry, either the strike ratchets (for example +$X per $Y of run-rate above $70,000 a month) or the premium steps up. A freeze with no expiry is how you donate the upside he described.

Joe’s counter will be: the right-hand columns are his volume, so the expected value is a tax on his work. That argument is half right. It is why you do not charge the full $244,000. It is not why you charge nothing. Three pieces of the freeze are not his pipeline:

  1. Exclusivity. Other buyers are frozen out for the tenor. That has a market price even if his volume is zero.
  2. Organic upside. The +10%/year path in section 4 already gets the book to $1.5 million without TradeMap. A $900,000 strike caps that too.
  3. Information and key-person time. He sees how the book is wired. Kim is the only IT person. Integration is a separate earned fee; the option still leaks operational detail.

A clean split: charge market custom for exclusivity and organic freeze (~5–10% of strike), not 100% of the expected value of his pipeline. That lands at $45,000–$90,000 before tenor adjustments. $75,000 sits in the middle.

9. Black-Scholes as a cross-check, not a price

Treat 60% of standing value as the spot (S = $720,000), strike K = $900,000 (25% out of the money), no dividend, 5% continuous risk-free. Private-company volatility is not observed; 40–60% is a normal small-business band, 80% is a “this book can double or die” band — which, for a four-month prop firm, is not crazy. Figures below are European calls. They ignore control, gates, and the fact that a large part of vol is Joe’s own effort. Use them as a map of tenor versus money, not as a term-sheet formula.

9 months~$51k~$75k~$99k~$149k
12 months~$68k~$97k~$125k~$180k

Three readings:

  • $75,000 is the 50% vol, 9-month cell. That is the honest centre: a young private book, freeze through integration and trial plus a short exercise window, not a year of his volume at a frozen price.
  • $150,000 is the 80% vol, 9-month cell, or ~60–80% vol at 12 months. That is the earlier top of the band. It is what you charge if the freeze runs into the volume phase with no ratchet. It is not what you open with after one call.
  • A 90-day freeze is a $20,000–$35,000 option at normal vol. If he will only lock a short window, do not still ask $75,000; drop the premium and drop the tenor together.

Because a slice of the volatility is his distribution, do not take the 80% column and add a $50,000 integration fee on top. That is how $200,000 of day-one cash appears, and how a serious counterparty leaves. Charge integration as work. Charge the option at the 40–60% vol column for the tenor you actually grant. Keep the 80% column as the price of a long, unracheted freeze.

10. Integration fee is not option premium

Kim said 2–3 weeks if the TAMS API matches; Joe planned 1–1.5 months. Kim is the only IT person, so that is the whole production book. Replacement cost for a senior Java/API engineer at about $200/hour × 160–200 hours is $32,000–$40,000 before sole-source, staging, and not breaking live payouts. Ask $50,000 USD (floor $35,000). Half on kickoff, half when Kim writes that the TAMS API is accepted. Overrun if their API does not match: $3,000/week after week 5, cap another $15,000. Trial stays free for his B2B partners, not free work by Verodus.

$15,000–$25,000 is cost recovery and tells him Verodus will carry the partnership. $80,000+ as integration-only looks like a toll. White-label SaaS “setup” fees of a few thousand dollars are irrelevant: those are plugging into a vendor’s product, not wiring a live custom backend to TAMS.

The integration fee is earned whether or not he later buys 60%. It does not credit against the $900,000 unless you choose to as a concession. The option premium does credit, and you keep it if he walks. Mixing the two into one number is fine commercially (one wire is easier). Mixing them conceptually is how you underprice the freeze.

Preferred: $50,000 integration + $75,000 option premium = $125,000 cash before the trial. Freeze 6–9 months from signing, or 90 days after written acceptance of the trial, whichever first. Premium credits to the $900,000. Integration does not. If he closes, he pays $825,000 more. If he walks, you have $125,000 for the work and the lock-up.

If he will only send one wire: $100,000 non-refundable, covers integration and the option, 6-month freeze. Credit the full $100,000 at close as a concession (net $800,000 more) so he is not paying twice for the same cheque. This is slightly cheaper for you than the two-cheque package and much easier for him to say yes to.

What “reasonable” means on each side of the table

Reasonable for Verodus to insist

Some cash on signing. A strike at or above $900,000 for a 6–9 month window, or a ratchet if the window runs into his volume. Kim’s time paid at $50,000, not absorbed. Premium kept if he walks. Cheque to Capital. Chun and Kim in the perimeter. No binding SPA with a walk right.

Reasonable for Joe to refuse

$150,000 premium plus $50,000 integration after one call. A strike that still ratchets up after he has already paid 10% option money. Paying for a 12-month freeze and then also giving a volume top-up. Refundable “premium.” Personal indemnities. KYC moving off LLC-FZ.

If he counters at $50,000 all-in with a 12-month $600,000 lock, that is not a near-miss. It is a free-option structure with a discounted strike. Counter with package C (short freeze, $900,000 ceiling) or package B (one $100,000 cheque, 6 months, $900,000). Do not split the difference to $600,000.

13. Negotiation order, and what would change the numbers

  1. Send the org chart and the two agreements. No numbers in that letter.
  2. Kim and Chun agree the perimeter and that cash lands at Capital.
  3. Term sheet, not an SPA: Package A or B, $900,000 strike, gates, 6–9 month long-stop, credit mechanics.
  4. If he wants 12 months into volume: add a ratchet or step up to Package D. Do not give him both a long freeze and a cheap premium.
  5. If he wants $600,000: missing $150,000–$300,000 is a ratchet on his volume, not a gift, and the option premium does not go to zero.

Cut $1.2 million standing if management accounts show $70,000 is not holding, or if ever-paid drifts toward 7% without a price rise. Cut the $900,000 ask if 1591011 or Kim is out of the perimeter — then you are not selling this asset. Raise both if you get a year of $70,000+ with ads on and a second engineer; at that point 5× earnings starts to be a conversation and $900,000 for 60% becomes cheap for him.

Raise the premium if the freeze lengthens. Drop the premium if the freeze shortens. Do not drop the premium because he says the upside is his. Charge for exclusivity and organic cap; let the strike at $900,000 (already 25% above pro-rata) be his compensation for bringing volume.

Carry into the huddle. Standing $1.2 million. Ask $900,000 cash for 60%. Take $750,000. Floor $660,000 with a ratchet. Option: $75,000 premium + $50,000 integration, or one $100,000 cheque. Freeze 6–9 months, then lapse. Credit premium to close; keep it if he walks. Not Hub. Not a binding SPA. Not a number in the first email.

The earlier $75,000–$150,000 premium range stays as a band. $75,000 is reasonable. $100,000 is reasonable if it is the only cheque. $150,000 is reasonable only for a 12-month unracheted freeze. $0 is not reasonable at any tenor once a price is locked.
