1. What this DCF is, and what it is not

This is an unlevered free-cash-flow (FCFF) model of the three affiliates as they stand, with ads restored, Kim in the chair, and no TradeMap volume in the base. Five explicit years from 25 August 2026, then a terminal value. Discount rate 25%. Mid-year convention on the explicit period; terminal discounted from the end of year 5. Terminal value is a 50/50 blend of a 3.5× exit on year-5 pretax leftover and a Gordon growth model on year-6 FCFF at 2.5% perpetual growth.

It is not a banker’s CIM. Four months of operating history is not a cycle. The discount rate does the work that a longer book would have done. Joe’s CSE / NASDAQ colour is not in any standing case. A DCF that capitalises his pipeline as if it were already contracted would be a marketing document.

No interest-bearing debt is modelled. Processor reserves are treated as working capital (2% of incremental receipts). Hosting sits inside opex. Maintenance capex is $10,000 in year 0, inflated at 3%. David at $1,500/month is an assumption for part-time cost; intern at $3,000/month is from the live run-rate. Tax is 15% blended — see §4.

7. The freeze, in DCF terms

If he exercises at $900,000 after the book has followed the Joe path, he is buying 60% of a $2.65 million DCF — $1.59 million of value — for $900,000. Payoff ≈ $690,000 in that case, plus whatever multiple expansion listing talk adds, which this model does not. If the book only does the organic-upside path, 60% is $941,000, and a $900,000 strike is barely in the money. If the book stays on the base path, 60% is $811,000 and the strike is out of the money — which is the right shape for a ceiling: he only exercises if the pie grew.

That is the DCF reason a one-year lock is not free. Price it with the option-premium paper: $75,000 for 6–9 months, $100,000 as a single cheque, $125,000–$150,000 only if the freeze runs 12 months into volume with no ratchet.

10. What would change the DCF

  • Management accounts showing $70,000 is not holding once ads restart — cut year-1 receipts, the model falls toward the downside case.
  • Ever-paid drifting toward 7% without a price rise — use the conservative or downside path as the new base.
  • 1591011 or Kim out of the perimeter — this DCF is not that asset. Licence risk is not in the 8%; it is a different company.
  • Accountant confirms 27% and no SBD — standing still ~$1.2m on the base path; expected value dips. Do not cut the ask solely for tax.
  • A year of $70k+ with ads on and a second engineer — drop 3 points of company-specific risk, and 5× year-5 leftover starts to be a conversation.
