# Entity and agreement map

How the three signed documents line up with what was said on 25 August 2026. Attach the PDFs when sending this to Joe.

## The three documents

| Document | Date | Parties | Core bargain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operational Services, Domain Usage and Evaluation Rights Agreement | 28 April 2026 | **Verodus Capital Inc.** (BC) ↔ **Verodus L.L.C.-FZ** (Dubai) | Capital markets, KYCs, and processes payments; Dubai licences the domain and evaluation/affiliate tools. Optional 5% royalty Dubai → Capital. |
| Software Sublicense and Data Licensing Agreement | 28 April 2026 | **Verodus Holding Inc.** ↔ **Verodus L.L.C.-FZ** | Holding sublicenses **third-party** software to Dubai for data collection. Dubai owns raw data; Holding gets aggregated insights. No cash. |
| Software Sublicense and Data Licensing Agreement | 31 May 2026 | **1591011 B.C. Ltd.** ↔ **Verodus L.L.C.-FZ** | 1591011 owns **pre-existing proprietary** software/CRM and sublicenses it to Dubai. Same data split as above. No cash. |

All three: English; governed by **British Columbia** law; disputes to **BCICAC** arbitration in Vancouver; 36-month term; 30-day cure then termination for material breach.

## 1. Software and system

**Call:** Built from scratch after leaving Exera. A BC company white-labels to Verodus Dubai. An investor holds that BC company. Kim is Director/CEO of the operating setup.

**Paper:**

- **1591011 B.C. Ltd.** is “owner and operator” of proprietary software, CRM, and related technology (31 May agreement, Recital A, s.1.4).
- Sublicense to Dubai is **non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable, limited** (s.2.1). **No modify, reverse-engineer, copy (except backup), or re-sublicense** (s.2.2).
- **Verodus Holding Inc.** separately sublicenses **third-party** software products it licenses from others (28 April software agreement, Recital A, s.1.4). Same use restrictions.
- On termination of either software agreement, the sublicense **ends immediately**. The licence of aggregated insights **survives**.

**Implication for a TAMS / TradeMap integration:** Dubai cannot re-sublicense the proprietary system to TradeMap or TAMS. Any third-party use needs 1591011 (and, for third-party tools, Holding / upstream vendors) in the chain, or a new licence.

## 2. Data

**Call:** Dubai collects data and runs CRM/dashboard. Trial data and future data would be “combined” and sit under “the company” if the parties grow together.

**Paper:**

- All **raw data** and **client-identifying** information is **sole and exclusive property of Verodus L.L.C.-FZ** (both software agreements, s.3.2).
- Dubai **may sell or license** raw or aggregated data to third parties at its sole discretion (s.3.4).
- 1591011 and Holding each receive a **perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, worldwide, non-exclusive** licence to aggregated **anonymised** insights only (s.3.3) — including the right to resell and sublicense those insights.
- Full set of aggregated insights is due to the software licensor **no later than 36 months** after each effective date; status reports every six months (s.4).

**Implication:** “Combined data under one company” is **not** what the current contracts say. A merger or joint go-to-market that pools trader data would need an amendment or a new data-processing / ownership agreement.

## 3. Domain, marketing, KYC

**Call:** Dubai owns the domain. Canada has the licence to use it and must send clients / market for Dubai.

**Paper (operational agreement):**

- Dubai owns `verodus.com` (Recital B).
- Capital gets a **non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable** licence to use the domain, run platform evaluations, and use Dubai’s affiliate software for marketing and traffic during the term (s.3.1–3.2).
- Capital must drive traffic to the domain, provide admin/KYC support, and perform its independent payment and evaluation activities (s.3.3, s.2.1–2.2).
- Capital has **no access** to Dubai’s software beyond the affiliate-tool licence, and **no operational control** of Dubai’s merchant accounts (s.2.3–2.4, s.12.2).
- Subscription targets were to be mutually agreed within six months of 28 April 2026, then reviewed on a rolling six-month basis (s.5). Status of those targets is not in the PDFs.

## 4. Money and tax posture

**Call:** Canada is the profit centre; ~95% of payments; 5% can go to Dubai as a discretionary royalty. UAE processor exists but unused. Joe noted Canada is easier for a listing vehicle, Dubai is better for tax, and current volume is small enough to leave as-is.

**Paper:**

- Capital processes pay-ins, rewards, refunds, and banking **on its own merchant accounts, for its own account** — not as a service to Dubai (s.2.2(a), s.2.3).
- Dubai is supposed to run **its own isolated** merchant accounts (deposits and same-method payouts only). **No commingling** (s.2.4, Recital D).
- Default: **no invoices and no payments** between Capital and Dubai (s.4.1).
- Exception: Dubai may impose a **5% royalty on Capital’s gross sales (CAD)** at its sole discretion, 90 days’ written notice, quarterly in arrears by the 15th (s.4.2).
- Both software agreements also recites that reciprocal licences are arm’s-length fair value for **Canadian and UAE transfer-pricing** purposes (software agreements s.5.3).

**Implication:** The 95/5 split Alexander described is the **optional royalty**, not an automatic waterfall. If the royalty has never been switched on, Canada currently keeps 100% of processed receipts. Joe’s counsel will likely ask which merchant account is live, which entity issues trader invoices/receipts, and how GST/HST, UAE VAT, and transfer pricing are actually filed.

## 5. Independence clause

Each agreement says it is **bilateral and entirely independent** of any other agreement between the parties or affiliates (operational s.12.1; software s.12.1). There is no single “Verodus group” services agreement that ties software + data + payments + brand into one stack. For a buyer, that means **four companies and three contracts**, not one target.
